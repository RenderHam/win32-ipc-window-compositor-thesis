## 📅 Phase-by-Phase Weekly Breakdown## Phase 1: Research, Foundations & Initial Hooking (Weeks 1–4)

* Week 1-2: Write the formal thesis proposal. Review existing literature on Windows desktop compositors, X11/Wayland architectures, and Win32 messaging loops.
* Week 3-4: Set up your development repository (C++ or Rust). Use AI to map out the core kernel and user libraries (user32.dll, kernel32.dll).
* Milestone: Proposal approved by your advisor; your development environment is functional.

## Phase 2: Core Engineering & Window Hijacking (Weeks 5–9)

* Week 5-6: Build the window finder module. Capture external target window handles (HWND) and apply basic style modifications (stripping title bars, borders, and close buttons).
* Week 7-9: Implement the naive SetParent loop. Write the geometric matrix algorithm that forces the child window to resize and snap to a container panel.
* Milestone: A standard application (like Notepad) is visually trapped inside your custom application shell.

## Phase 3: Advanced Systems Programming & Focus Routing (Weeks 10–14)

* Week 10-12: Build the Low-Level Input Hooking State Machine (WH_MOUSE_LL and WH_KEYBOARD_LL). Force Windows to route keystrokes into the nested child window without stripping active focus from your parent application.
* Week 13-14: Implement a Shared Memory IPC Ring Buffer. Program the system to handle real-time, zero-delay window resizing messages between processes to eliminate layout flickering.
* Milestone: The nested application is fully interactive, types smoothly, and matches your parent application's layout modifications perfectly.

## Phase 4: Edge Cases, Security Barriers & Cross-API Tweaks (Weeks 15–18)

* Week 15-16: Address hardware acceleration gaps. Modify window styles and paint flags (WM_PAINT, WM_ERASEBKGND) to accommodate more demanding Electron apps (like VS Code).
* Week 17-18: Build a basic elevated background service (Broker) to duplicate handles and test parenting across standard and Administrator integrity levels.
* Milestone: Your engine successfully embeds a diverse profile of simple and complex applications.

## Phase 5: Executing the Test Matrix & Benchmarking (Weeks 19–21)

* Week 19: Run your standardized test cases across GDI, Electron, and heavy graphics apps to log your official "Pass/Fail" percentages.
* Week 20-21: Profile performance metrics. Use software profilers to measure exact input propagation delays (in microseconds), frame-rate stability (FPS), and CPU/RAM overhead bytes.
* Milestone: All quantitative charts, graphs, and failure-analysis tables are finalized for your data chapter.

## Phase 6: Final Paper Compilation & Defense Prep (Weeks 22–24)

* Week 22-23: Complete the written manuscript (typically 50–80 pages), polishing the Methodology, Results, and Technical Limitations chapters. Use AI to refine your academic tone.
* Week 24: Submit your final draft, create your presentation slide deck, and practice answering aggressive panel questions regarding your OS architectural gaps.
* Milestone: Graduation.
